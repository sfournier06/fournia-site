An artificial intelligence consultant in Quebec in 2026 costs between $75 and $250 per hour as an independent, or a flat fee of $100 to $15,000 per project depending on the depth of the work. Three formats dominate the market: the audit ($100 to $2,000), the implementation of an AI automation ($500 to $15,000) and the monthly maintenance retainer ($300 to $1,500/month for a small business). For comparison, a salaried AI analyst in Montreal costs between $31.73 and $96 per hour according to [Job Bank (Guichet-Emplois)](https://www.guichetemplois.gc.ca/rapportmarche/salaire-profession/24508/22446) (in French), which places consultant rates in a logic of specialization and risk taken on. The federal IRIA program, administered by Canada Economic Development for Quebec Regions (DEC), can fund up to 50% of authorized costs for an eligible small business, as a repayable contribution, which spreads out the cost of the project.

## Why do prices vary so much from one AI consultant to another?

Three factors explain the price gap between two consultants who seem to be selling the same thing.

The first is the **type of provider**. A solo consultant, like FournIA on Montreal's North Shore, carries minimal fixed costs and bills for results. A boutique of 5 to 15 people adds a layer of project management and specialization. A large agency also bills for its offices, its sales teams and its brand. For the same deliverable, the gap can reach a factor of 2 to 5.

The second is the **depth of the project**. A scoping audit of a few hours is not comparable to an implementation that touches 3 pieces of software, the OpenAI or Claude API, N8N webhooks and team training. The price follows the scope, not just the time.

The third is the **consultant's business model**. Some sell time (hourly rate). Others sell a result (flat fee). Others still sell a transformation (monthly retainer or partnership). The more risk the consultant takes on for the result, the higher the rate.

## How much does an AI audit cost in Quebec in 2026?

An AI audit costs between $100 and $2,000 for a Quebec small business, depending on depth and the size of the company.

A light audit (1 to 3 hours) quickly maps 1 or 2 processes and points to the first automation to build. Typical rate: $100 to $500. This is the range FournIA charges for small businesses that want a clear starting point with no commitment.

A full audit (1 to 3 days of work) includes a map of all processes, a quantified ROI analysis per use case, a 6 to 12 month roadmap and a stack recommendation. Typical rate: $1,000 to $2,000.

An audit that costs $0 is almost always a sales pitch in disguise. That can be fine, but you should know it: a free audit is not neutral.

## How much does implementing an AI solution cost for a small business?

Implementing an AI solution for a Quebec small business costs between $500 and $15,000 for a first project, and generally takes 2 to 12 weeks.

A few concrete benchmarks:

| Type of project | Timeline | Price range |
|---|---|---|
| Automating a single flow (e.g. incoming emails sorted and summarized) | 2 to 3 weeks | $500 to $2,500 |
| AI agent that answers customer questions about a product | 3 to 6 weeks | $2,000 to $7,000 |
| Overhaul of a complete process (quotes, invoicing, follow-up) | 6 to 12 weeks | $5,000 to $15,000 |
| Custom multi-department project | 12+ weeks | $15,000 and up |

The price does not cover only development. It includes the information meeting, the mapping, the build, testing, installation, training and an initial bug safety net. An implementation without training is a promise of failure: an AI system nobody uses generates no ROI.

## What is a monthly retainer and how much does it cost?

A monthly retainer in AI consulting costs between $300 and $1,500/month for a Quebec small business, and goes higher for strategic partnership projects that include continuous building.

A retainer typically covers three things: maintenance of the automations in production (bug fixes, API updates, handling changes at providers), support and adjustment hours, and regular follow-up to propose the next automation.

A retainer becomes useful once a small business has 2 or 3 AI systems in production. Before that, a one-off project is enough. After that, not having a retainer creates operational risk: an API changes, an account disconnects, and a critical process goes down with nobody there to catch it.

## How much does an AI consultant cost per hour in Quebec?

The hourly rate of an AI consultant in Quebec in 2026 is between $75 and $250 per hour for an independent or a boutique. Large agencies can go above $300 per hour.

To put these figures in perspective, a salaried AI analyst in Montreal costs between $31.73 and $96 per hour according to [Job Bank (Guichet-Emplois)](https://www.guichetemplois.gc.ca/rapportmarche/salaire-profession/24508/22446) (in French) (loaded salary). Hiring an in-house analyst therefore costs less per hour, but the small business then pays for 100% of their time, even when there is no active AI project. A consultant is paid for real needs.

Cases where the consultant is more cost-effective:
- First AI project with no in-house expertise.
- A one-off need for specialized expertise (RAG, agents, a specific N8N integration).
- Not enough volume to justify a full-time position.

Cases where hiring becomes more cost-effective:
- More than 25 to 30 hours per week of recurring need.
- A structural AI roadmap over several years.

## Does the DEC-IRIA grant really change the math?

The IRIA program can fund up to 50% of the authorized costs of an AI project for a Quebec small business. For a small business, it is a **repayable contribution** (it is generally non-repayable only for a non-profit), and costs are only eligible from the date the application is filed. The program is administered by Canada Economic Development for Quebec Regions (DEC), under the official name of [Regional Artificial Intelligence Initiative](https://www.canada.ca/fr/developpement-economique-regions-quebec/financement-services/initiative-regionale-en-matiere-dintelligence-artificielle.html) (in French).

In practice, an $8,000 implementation project can drop to about $4,000 net for the small business. That changes two things:
1. The ROI calculation is done on half the cost, so the break-even point comes quickly.
2. The project becomes accessible to small businesses that would have hesitated at full price.

A serious AI consultant in Quebec knows the program and helps with filing the application **before** the contract is signed. At FournIA, this is a non-negotiable rule: the grant application is filed before signing when it applies.

## How do I know if the price is justified?

An AI consultant's price is justified when three conditions are met.

**The consultant quantifies the expected ROI** in hours saved, added sales or costs avoided. If the proposal only talks about hours and features, that is a red flag.

**The rate card is clear and transparent.** A consultant who changes their price depending on the client has no delivery standard. A public rate card signals a standardized service.

**The project includes training and Law 25 compliance.** A serious AI consultant builds compliance with Quebec's privacy law (Law 25), which governs the protection of personal information and is overseen by the Commission d'accès à l'information du Québec, into the project, especially if it processes client data. It is not a disclaimer: it is a concrete step that protects the small business.

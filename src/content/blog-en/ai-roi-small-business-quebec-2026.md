## What is the ROI of AI for a small business in Quebec?

Quebec small businesses that have completed an automation project report a median productivity gain of 17%, and 62% of them recovered their investment in less than three years, according to a study of 353 small businesses that are CFIB members and Investissement Québec clients (October 2025). This ROI varies widely by type of project: a simple automation (email processing, customer follow-ups) can pay for itself in a few weeks, while a custom AI agent generally takes 3 to 6 months. The key is to choose the right first project, not to automate everything at once.

The rest of this article breaks down these figures, shows how to calculate a realistic ROI before committing a single dollar, and lays out the pitfalls that derail an otherwise promising project.

## What do Quebec figures say about AI ROI in 2026?

The joint [CFIB and Investissement Québec report from October 2025](https://www.cfib-fcei.ca/hubfs/FCEI-IQ%20rapport%20Final-2025-1.pdf) (in French) remains the most solid data for a small business in Quebec. Out of 353 businesses surveyed, the median productivity gain after an automation project is 17%, and 62% recover their costs in less than three years.

BDC points the same way in its [April 2026 press release](https://www.bdc.ca/fr/a-propos/centre-des-medias/communiques-presse/bdc-lance-lift-pour-aider-pme-a-passer-intention-a-action-en-ia) (in French): Canadian small businesses that use AI show productivity 24% higher than those that do not. The gap is not marginal, it is structural.

Still, adoption remains low. The [Institut de la statistique du Québec](https://statistique.quebec.ca/en/produit/publication/adoption-et-utilisation-intelligence-artificielle-entreprises-au-quebec-2024-2025) puts the share of Quebec businesses using AI in production in the second quarter of 2025 at 12.7%. Statistics Canada confirms the trend: [12.2% of Canadian businesses](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-621-m/11-621-m2025008-fra.htm) (in French) in Q2 2025, compared with 6.1% a year earlier. The window for a competitive advantage is still open.

## How long does a small business take to recover its investment?

The timeline depends on the complexity of the project. Investissement Québec [reports that nearly half of micro-businesses](https://grandv.investquebec.com/contenus-a-decouvrir/articles/lautomatisation-comme-levier-pour-augmenter-la-productivite-des-pme-quebecoises) (in French) recover the cost of their project in less than a year, which is consistent with the following observation: the smaller the business, the more direct its processes are and the faster they are to automate.

Three realistic orders of magnitude for planning:

- **Simple automation** (payment reminders, sorting incoming emails, generating standardized replies): payback in a few weeks to 3 months.
- **Intermediate automation** (lead qualification, extracting data from PDFs, pipeline tracking): 3 to 6 months.
- **Custom AI agent** (assistant connected to several systems, generating complex documents): 6 to 12 months, sometimes more.

A common fear is job losses. The data contradicts it: Statistics Canada reports that 89.4% of Canadian businesses did not reduce their headcount after adopting AI. The gain goes into capacity, not cuts.

## How do you calculate the ROI of an automation before buying it?

An honest ROI calculation rests on four measurable elements.

1. **Total project cost**: initial build plus annual recurring costs (licences, API, maintenance).
2. **Hours saved per week**: multiplied by the loaded hourly rate of the person involved (salary plus payroll costs), then annualized over 50 weeks.
3. **Indirect gains**: additional sales, errors avoided, shorter response times, better retention. These gains are often larger than the hours saved, but harder to put a number on.
4. **Applicable grants**: the [IRIA program from Canada Economic Development](https://www.canada.ca/fr/developpement-economique-regions-quebec/financement-services/initiative-regionale-en-matiere-dintelligence-artificielle.html) (in French) can fund up to 50% of the authorized costs of an AI adoption project; for a small business, it is a repayable contribution, and costs are only eligible from the date the application is filed. This lever changes the calculation.

A concrete example. An administrative assistant spends 6 hours per week sorting emails and following up on accounts receivable, at a loaded rate of $35/h. Gross annual gain: 6 x 35 x 50 = $10,500. If the automation costs $4,000 to build plus $600 per year in licences, the net ROI for year 1 is $5,900, a payback of about 5 months. With the DEC-IRIA grant, the payback drops below 3 months.

## Which types of AI projects give the best returns?

The projects with the highest ROI share three traits: high frequency of execution, low variation between cases, and a direct link to revenue or cash flow.

The quick-return areas observed in Quebec small businesses are automated sales follow-ups, incoming email processing with smart routing, lead qualification, extracting data from invoices and PDFs, accounts receivable reminders, and automatic analysis of repetitive documents (orders, estimates, quotes).

The projects that often disappoint: poorly scoped external chatbots, predictive dashboards without clean data, and internal tools nobody uses after launch. The cause is almost never the technology. It is the choice of project and adoption.

## Why do some projects miss their ROI?

The failures observed come down to three recurring causes. First, a poorly chosen project: low frequency of use or a task too variable to be automated cleanly. Second, incomplete adoption: the tool is delivered without training, and the team goes back to its old methods within two weeks. Third, a scope that is too ambitious from the start: aiming for a complete AI agent when a simple workflow would have been enough and paid off faster.

The most reliable method is to start with a fast-payback project (less than 6 months), measure the result, then expand. This discipline keeps an AI budget from turning into an emotional expense.

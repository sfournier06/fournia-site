## How to train your employees on AI in a Quebec small business?

To train your employees on AI in a Quebec small business, a single block of a few hours can be enough to get started, as long as it is built on the team's real tasks rather than on theory. Each person should leave with three concrete uses tied to their job, a tool approved by the company and a one-page usage rule that clearly says what never to give to AI, especially client information. A team review about 30 days later lets you keep what works and fix the rest.

An administrative assistant who wants to write emails faster or an estimator who structures quotes needs practical guidance and clear limits.

## Why train your employees on AI now?

AI adoption is still a minority, but training tops the plans of businesses that intend to adopt it. In the 12 months before the second quarter of 2025, 12.2% of Canadian businesses reported using AI to produce goods or deliver services, according to [Statistics Canada](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-621-m/11-621-m2025011-fra.htm) (in French). In Quebec, 12.7% of businesses used AI for production purposes, and the share rises to 26.1% among businesses with 100 or more employees ([Institut de la statistique du Québec](https://statistique.quebec.ca/fr/produit/publication/adoption-et-utilisation-intelligence-artificielle-entreprises-au-quebec-2024-2025) (in French)).

Among businesses that plan to use AI in the next 12 months, Statistics Canada observes that the most expected operational change is training current employees to use AI: "About half of businesses (49.8%) reported that they intend to train their staff to use AI" ([Statistics Canada](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-621-m/11-621-m2025011-fra.htm) (in French)). Among businesses that do not plan to use AI in the next 12 months, the same publication notes, among the other reasons given, a lack of knowledge about AI's capabilities (11.3%) and concerns about privacy and security (8.1%). A short training that covers the tool's capabilities and the privacy rules addresses both of these concerns.

Job exposure also weighs in. About 59% of Quebec's workforce, or about 2,700,000 people, held a job with high exposure to AI in 2024, according to the [Institut de la statistique du Québec](https://statistique.quebec.ca/fr/communique/majorite-professions-quebec-hautement-exposees-intelligence-artificielle) (in French). The ISQ measures this exposure with an index of potential occupational exposure to AI adjusted for complementarity.

## What are the 4 steps to train a team on AI?

Here is a simple method a small business can apply without a training department or a technical team.

1. **Choose one task per role.** Before the training, ask each person to name a repetitive task in their week: writing emails, summarizing meetings, preparing a quote, looking up information. The training is built from these real tasks. To spot good candidates, see our article on [the tasks to automate first with AI](/en/blog/tasks-to-automate-first-ai-2026/).
2. **Approve a single tool.** The whole team uses the same tool, configured by the company, rather than a mix of personal accounts. Check the privacy settings, including the option that allows conversations to be used to train the model, and turn it off when it exists.
3. **Write a one-page usage rule.** It says which tool to use, for which tasks, what never goes into a request and who to check with when in doubt. A short page is more likely to be read and applied than a long document.
4. **Hold a review after 30 days.** Bring the team together to see which uses held up, which were dropped and what questions came up. Adjust the usage rule as needed and add one or two uses if the team is ready.

## What should an AI usage rule contain?

The usage rule is the most important piece of the training, because it protects the company after the trainer leaves. It fits on one page and answers four questions:

- **Which tool?** The name of the approved tool and how to access it.
- **For what?** The permitted uses: drafts, summaries, rewording, brainstorming.
- **Never what?** No personal information in requests: client name, contact details, file, financial or health information. No confidential company information either.
- **Who checks?** Someone always rereads the result before it goes to a client.

In Quebec, the province's privacy law (Law 25) governs the protection of personal information, and an AI tool that receives client data sends that information outside your systems. The rule "no personal information in requests" is the simplest reflex to teach and to follow. To go further on consent, hosting and obligations, read our guide on [Law 25 and AI in business](/en/blog/law-25-ai-compliance-quebec-2026/).

## Free or custom AI training: which to choose?

Both have their place, and they do not meet the same need. The free platform from [HEC Montréal](https://www.hec.ca/salle-de-presse/communiques/2024/implanter-ia-en-entreprise-nouvelle-plateforme-en-francais) (in French) offers four tracks in French: What is AI?, How does AI work?, How to implement AI in an organization? and What are the issues with AI?

In our reading of these four tracks, they teach what AI is, how it works and what is at stake. It is a good foundation for a manager who wants to understand the subject. They do not show your receptionist how to answer emails faster with the tool your company approved, while following your usage rule.

| Criterion | Free resource (e.g. HEC) | Custom training |
|---|---|---|
| Content | Understanding AI and its issues | The team's real tasks |
| Tool | General | The tool approved by the company |
| Usage rule | Not included | Written for the company |
| Pace | Individual, self-directed | As a group, in one block |
| Cost | Free | Paid |

A combination works well: the manager takes a free track to understand the subject, then the team gets a hands-on workshop on its own tasks.

## What mistakes to avoid when training employees on AI?

The mistakes to avoid mostly come from training that is too broad or too abstract.

- **Touring 15 tools.** Employees may come out impressed without adopting any of them. A single tool, well mastered, gets more results.
- **Teaching theory without tasks.** A demo on a made-up example does not transfer to the workstation. Exercises should use the team's real documents, once personal information has been removed.
- **Forgetting the usage rule.** Without written limits, each employee decides alone what to paste into the tool, including client data.
- **Training once, then never mentioning it again.** Without a review after 30 days, the uses fade and bad habits set in.
- **Training before choosing the first use case.** If the company does not yet know where AI helps it, the starting point is elsewhere: see [where to start with AI in a small business](/en/blog/where-to-start-ai-small-business-quebec-2026/).

## FAQ

### How long does it take to train employees on AI?
A single block of a few hours can be enough to get started, as long as you work on the team's real tasks. The goal of that block is for each person to leave with three concrete uses they can apply quickly. The rest is learned through practice, with a team review about 30 days later.

### Do employees need to know AI already before the training?
No. Good workplace training starts from the tasks each person already does (emails, quotes, summaries, research) and shows how AI fits into them. No technical knowledge is required. Employees who already use ChatGPT or a similar tool still benefit: they learn the limits, the settings and what should never go into a request.

### Which AI tool should I choose to train a team, and with what settings?
Choose a single approved tool for the whole team (for example ChatGPT, Claude or Copilot), ideally in a business or team version rather than scattered personal accounts. Check in the settings whether conversations can be used to train the model and turn that option off when it exists. A single tool simplifies training, support and compliance with the usage rule.

### What should you never give to AI, under Law 25?
The simplest rule for a Quebec small business: no personal information in requests made to AI (client name, address, phone number, file, financial or health information), unless the tool and the use have been approved and governed. Law 25 governs the protection of personal information in Quebec. Anonymizing or removing that information before pasting text into an AI tool is the basic reflex to teach.

### Is HEC Montréal's free training enough to train a team?
HEC Montréal's free platform offers four tracks: what AI is, how it works, how to implement it in an organization and what its issues are. In our reading, these tracks do a good job of teaching what AI is and what is at stake. They do not replace a workshop on your team's real tasks, with your tools and your usage rule.

### How do I measure whether the AI training worked after 30 days?
Hold a team review about 30 days after the training. Ask each person which uses they kept, which they dropped and why, and what questions came up about the usage rule. A use that is kept and used every week is the best sign that the training took hold.

### Is in-person or video AI training better?
Both work. In person encourages discussion and lets you work directly at the team's workstations, which helps employees who are less comfortable with technology. Video works well for distributed teams or follow-ups. For a small business in Blainville, Laval or Montreal, on-site training is a simple option to organize.

## How to choose a good AI consultant for a small business in Quebec?

To choose a good AI consultant in Quebec, check five things before signing: that they diagnose your processes before proposing a solution, that they talk about your business before talking about technology, that they include training for your team, that they explain where your data will be hosted (Law 25 compliance), and that they stay accountable after delivery. Insist on a paid audit that examines your real processes before any implementation contract: it is the best filter against an automation that does not fix your real pain point. Be wary of a consultant who promises a guaranteed result without having seen your operations, or who sells a technology before understanding your problem. In Quebec, two things change the math: the IRIA grant from Canada Economic Development for Quebec Regions can cover part of the eligible costs according to the program's terms (to be confirmed with a DEC advisor), and Quebec's privacy law (Law 25) sets specific rules on protecting your clients' personal information.

## Five criteria to evaluate an AI consultant

These five criteria separate a consultant who solves a problem from a technology salesperson.

1. **They diagnose before proposing.** A good consultant examines your real processes before naming a solution. If they propose a tool on the first call, without understanding how you work, they are selling a product, not an answer to your need.

2. **They talk about your business before talking about technology.** Technical vocabulary (models, APIs, workflows) should come after understanding your pain point. A consultant who starts with jargon has the priorities backwards ([Horizon IA](https://www.horizon-ia.io/blog/consultant-ia-pme) (in French)).

3. **They train your team.** An automation that nobody in your company knows how to use or fix is a risk, not a gain. Training should be part of the project, not an option billed on the side.

4. **They are clear about your data.** They tell you where it is hosted, what goes through external AI services, and how they comply with Law 25. A data processing agreement specifying who processes what is standard practice among serious providers ([Stema Partners](https://www.stemapartners.com/blog/choisir-agence-ia/) (in French)).

5. **They stay around after delivery.** Ask what happens when an automation breaks or a need changes. A consultant who delivers and then disappears leaves you with a system you cannot maintain.

## What questions to ask on the first call?

These questions quickly reveal whether you are dealing with a practitioner or a salesperson.

- How do you decide which process to automate first in my business?
- Can you give me an example of a project similar to mine and its result?
- Where will my data be hosted, and what information leaves Quebec?
- Is training for my team included in the price?
- What happens if the automation stops working in three months?
- Do you do an audit before proposing a contract, and how much does it cost?

## What red flags to recognize?

Three signals should make you slow down before signing.

- **No prior diagnosis.** The consultant proposes a solution without mapping your processes. They are guessing your need instead of measuring it ([Horizon IA](https://www.horizon-ia.io/blog/consultant-ia-pme) (in French)).
- **Technology before business.** They talk about tools and models before understanding how you make your money. Technology is a means, not the starting point.
- **No training planned.** The project delivers a system, but nobody on your side learns how to use it or monitor it.

Add a fourth signal: the promise of a guaranteed result without having seen your operations. Nobody can promise a precise number before measuring your starting point.

## The IRIA grant: part of your eligible costs

The Regional Artificial Intelligence Initiative (IRIA) from Canada Economic Development for Quebec Regions can cover **part of the eligible costs** of an AI adoption project, according to the program's terms ([ARF Québec / DEC](https://www.arfquebec.com/fr/accompagnement-details/initiative-regionale-en-matiere-dintelligence-artificielle-dec) (in French)).

In practice, part of what you pay a consultant for an AI project can be covered by this support. The exact percentage, eligibility, covered costs, deadline and application process must be confirmed with a DEC advisor before you commit: the precise terms depend on your situation.

IRIA is not the only support available in Quebec. There is a whole set of funding programs for AI adoption by small businesses, and a consultant who knows the landscape can point you to the one that fits your project ([Hellodarwin](https://hellodarwin.com/fr/aide-aux-entreprises/subventions-et-financement/intelligence-artificielle/quebec) (in French)). A good reflex: ask the consultant whether they file the grant application before signing, because some programs must be applied for before work begins.

## Law 25 and your AI consultant

As soon as an automation touches personal information (client emails, files, CRM contacts), Law 25 applies. This Quebec law governs the collection, use and protection of personal information, and it places obligations on your business, not only on your provider.

The most common blind spot is transfer outside Quebec. Most AI services (like large language models) process data on servers abroad. If personal information is sent there without being de-identified, you are transferring data outside Quebec, which triggers specific obligations under Law 25.

A serious consultant explains how they manage this risk: de-identification before sending data to an external service, consent validation, and a compliance document you can present. At FournIA, an AI consultancy for Quebec small businesses based on Montreal's North Shore, every non-trivial implementation goes through a Law 25 checkpoint that produces a compliance document handed to the client. Ask any consultant you are evaluating for the equivalent: if they do not have one, that is a signal to consider.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between an AI consultant and an AI agency in Quebec?**
An AI consultant is often a practitioner who diagnoses, builds and implements the work personally, with direct contact. An agency splits the work between several people and handles more volume, often at a higher price. For a small business, the real criterion is knowing who actually builds and who stays accountable after delivery, whatever the label.

**What questions should I ask on a first call with an AI consultant?**
Ask how they diagnose your needs before proposing anything, which process they would automate first, where your data will be hosted, whether training for your team is included, and what happens after delivery. A good consultant talks about your business before talking about technology.

**What red flags should I avoid when choosing an AI consultant?**
Three warning signs: no prior diagnosis of your processes, a pitch focused on technology before your business, and no training planned for your team. A consultant who promises a guaranteed result without having seen your operations is also a signal to take seriously.

**Should I pay for an audit before signing an implementation contract?**
A paid audit is a good sign: it forces the consultant to look at your real processes before proposing a solution. It reduces the risk of paying for an automation that does not fix your real pain point. At FournIA, an AI audit costs between $100 and $500 depending on scope.

**How do I know if an AI consultant complies with Law 25 and protects my client data?**
Ask where your data will be hosted, whether personal information is sent to AI services outside Quebec, and how your clients' consent is handled. A serious consultant can give you a document explaining these choices. Law 25 governs the protection of personal information in Quebec.

**Can I get a grant to pay for an AI consultant in Quebec?**
Yes. The Regional Artificial Intelligence Initiative (IRIA) from Canada Economic Development for Quebec Regions (DEC) can cover part of the eligible costs according to the program's terms. The exact percentage, eligibility and deadline must be confirmed with a DEC advisor.

**What is the difference between configuring an AI tool (like Zapier) and building a custom automation?**
Configuring an existing tool links applications with prebuilt rules: it is fast and inexpensive, but limited to the cases the tool was designed for. A custom automation is built for your exact processes, handles exceptions and evolves with you. The right choice depends on how complex your need is.

**How long before I see results with an AI consultant?**
It depends on the scope of the project and how quickly you provide access and documents. A targeted automation can be delivered in a few weeks. The main source of delay is not the building, it is response time and access on the small business's side.
